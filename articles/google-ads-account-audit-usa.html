US Google Ads Audit

Google Ads account audit checklist for US businesses

Updated September 28, 2026. Use this framework before a major budget change, agency handoff, tracking migration, or campaign rebuild. It is designed to produce a prioritized decision log, not a cosmetic score.

Start with the decision, not the dashboard

An audit should explain what is broken, what evidence supports that conclusion, what should change first, and how the change will be measured. A rising click-through rate is not automatically useful if qualified leads, sales, or contribution margin are falling. Record the business objective, acceptable acquisition cost, sales cycle, geographic scope, and reporting window before reviewing individual campaigns.

Export the current campaign settings, conversion actions, search terms, change history, billing totals, and key reports before editing anything. This creates a baseline and prevents the audit itself from destroying evidence.

1. Confirm account control and security

  • The advertiser has direct administrator access through a company-controlled identity.
  • Every user and linked manager is recognized, current, and assigned the minimum necessary role.
  • Two-step verification is enforced where available; shared passwords are not used.
  • The payments profile, Analytics property, Tag Manager container, Merchant Center, CRM, domain, call tracking, and creative files each have a documented controller.
  • Change history is reviewed for unexplained budget, URL, targeting, conversion, or user-access changes.

Resolve ownership and access before optimization. Otherwise a business may improve an account it cannot safely retain or hand off.

2. Audit conversion measurement

List every conversion action and classify it as primary, secondary, imported, website, call, store, or platform-hosted. Primary actions should represent outcomes the bidding system is genuinely expected to optimize. Page views, button clicks, and duplicate form events should not quietly carry the same decision weight as qualified leads or completed purchases.

  • Test forms, calls, purchases, values, currencies, consent states, and duplicate prevention.
  • Compare Google Ads, analytics, CRM, and finance totals using the same date and attribution assumptions.
  • Check whether enhanced conversions or offline imports are implemented with appropriate consent and data handling.
  • Verify that leads progress to accepted lead, opportunity, sale, and revenue stages.
  • Document the expected delay between click, conversion, qualification, and revenue.

A tracking discrepancy is an investigation prompt, not proof that one platform is wrong. Identify scope, timing, attribution, and implementation differences before changing bids.

3. Reconcile spend, budget, and business capacity

Match Google invoices to reported media spend and separate advertising charges from management, creative, software, and tracking fees. Review daily budget changes, limited-by-budget status, shared budgets, campaign seasonality, and geographic distribution. A campaign can meet a platform target while sending more demand than the sales team can process or shifting spend away from the most profitable service line.

  • Compare spend and qualified outcomes by campaign, state, metro, device, day, and hour.
  • Flag abrupt changes that lack a note in the change history or decision log.
  • Identify campaigns constrained by budget only after validating conversion quality.
  • Set a test budget and stop condition for uncertain expansion rather than assuming forecasts are guarantees.

4. Inspect search demand and traffic quality

Review actual search terms, not only keyword lists. Classify terms by business intent, location, product or service fit, funnel stage, and outcome quality. Look for spend on research queries, jobs, support requests, unrelated meanings, excluded locations, existing customers, and services the business does not provide.

Negative keywords should reduce irrelevant traffic without blocking valuable variants. Match type changes must be judged with conversion quality and search-term evidence. Segment branded, non-branded, competitor, and remarketing traffic so one easy-to-convert group does not hide weak acquisition performance elsewhere.

5. Review bidding and recommendations critically

Check whether the bid strategy has enough recent, correctly measured conversion data for its goal. Review target changes, learning periods, portfolio strategies, seasonality adjustments, value rules, and conversion goals at the account and campaign levels. Avoid changing targets, budgets, creative, and landing pages simultaneously because the result becomes difficult to interpret.

Google states that its Recommendations page uses account history, settings, and trends to generate customized suggestions. Treat each suggestion as an input for review. Confirm business fit, measurement quality, likely tradeoffs, and a rollback plan before applying it. Do not use optimization score as a substitute for profit or lead-quality evidence.

6. Check ads, assets, destinations, and policy status

  • Map each ad group to a clear search intent and a relevant landing page.
  • Check final URLs, tracking templates, redirects, mobile rendering, forms, calls, and page speed.
  • Review responsive search ad combinations and asset coverage without claiming that asset strength predicts business results.
  • Compare message, offer, eligibility, pricing, and location claims with the destination page.
  • Resolve disapprovals through the stated policy and appeal process; do not use account rotation, cloaking, or misleading business information.

7. Turn findings into a 30-day action plan

Rank findings by business impact, confidence, implementation effort, and reversibility. Separate urgent controls from experiments. Access compromise, broken primary conversion tracking, incorrect destinations, material policy issues, and runaway spend require immediate action. Keyword expansion, new creative, audience tests, and landing-page experiments can follow after measurement is stable.

  1. Preserve the baseline and assign an owner to every action.
  2. Fix critical access, billing, policy, and measurement defects.
  3. Remove clearly irrelevant traffic with reviewed exclusions.
  4. Run one interpretable test at a time where practical.
  5. Review qualified outcomes and revenue after the appropriate conversion lag.
  6. Record what changed, why, when, and what would trigger reversal.

Evidence to request from an auditor

A useful audit should provide dated exports or screenshots, the scope reviewed, assumptions, material limitations, prioritized findings, estimated effort, dependencies, and a measurement plan. It should distinguish verified defects from hypotheses. Be cautious when an audit promises a fixed performance result, recommends rebuilding everything without preserving history, or withholds the evidence unless management services are purchased.
