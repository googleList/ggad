Google Ads Governance

Who should own your Google Ads account?

Updated September 28, 2026. This guide helps US businesses separate Google Ads account access, manager-account ownership, billing control, and data custody before hiring an agency or consultant.

The practical answer

The advertiser should retain direct administrative access to the client account, use a business-controlled email identity, and understand which legal entity is attached to the payments profile. An agency normally works through its Google Ads manager account or an invited user rather than asking for the advertiser's password.

Google uses the word "ownership" in a specific manager-account context. A manager can be granted ownership privileges over a client account, but Google states that this does not remove the client account's data or administrative rights. Because the owner manager can manage users and other managers, that privilege should be granted only when the work genuinely requires it.

Four controls that should not be confused

  • Client account access: who can sign in, view campaigns, edit campaigns, manage users, or review billing.
  • Manager-account link: an agency or consultant can manage the client account from a separate manager account. Linking an existing account does not grant manager ownership by default.
  • Payments profile: the legal name, address, account type, tax information, payment methods, and authorized contacts associated with payment activity.
  • Connected assets: analytics properties, tag containers, merchant accounts, CRM integrations, call tracking, landing pages, audiences, and creative files have separate access controls.

A business can have campaign access while lacking control of the payments profile or analytics property. Review each layer instead of treating one admin invitation as complete ownership.

Recommended access model

  1. Create or confirm the client account under a company-controlled identity.
  2. Keep at least two current internal administrators where staffing permits.
  3. Use individual user accounts; do not share a common password.
  4. Link the agency manager account and grant the minimum permissions needed for the agreed scope.
  5. Keep the advertiser's legal details and payment method in the correct payments profile.
  6. Record who owns Analytics, Tag Manager, Merchant Center, the website, domain, call tracking, and CRM.
  7. Require two-step verification and review access on a schedule.

A manager-account link can provide the operational access needed for campaign work while preserving the advertiser's direct access. Grant manager ownership only after understanding the additional user-management and security privileges.

Billing profile questions to resolve before launch

Google describes the payments profile as the place that stores the legally responsible payer, payment methods, account type, tax information, and payment contacts. It may be shared across more than one Google product, so an edit can have effects beyond one Ads account.

  • Which legal entity is responsible for payment?
  • Is the profile an organization or an individual profile?
  • Who has Ads billing access, and who is a payments-profile administrator?
  • Will media spend be charged directly by Google to the advertiser?
  • Who receives invoices, payment failures, tax documents, and verification notices?
  • What happens to billing when the agency relationship ends?

Changing who pays can require a billing transfer and the appropriate Ads or payments permissions. Do not assume the name or country can be casually changed after campaigns begin.

Agency handoff checklist

  • Confirm the advertiser has working admin access before notice is given.
  • Export current users and manager links, then identify any unfamiliar access.
  • Confirm the active payments profile and backup payment method.
  • Preserve campaign history, change history, shared assets, negatives, audiences, scripts, and conversion definitions.
  • Transfer or retain access to Analytics, Tag Manager, Merchant Center, CRM, call tracking, domain, and landing pages.
  • Document automated rules, data feeds, offline imports, dashboards, and recurring reports.
  • Remove former-provider access only after replacement workflows are verified.

Starting a new account can discard useful history and learning. A clean handoff is usually preferable when the existing account legitimately belongs to the advertiser and its data can be retained.

Warning signs in a proposal

  • The provider will not give the advertiser direct administrative access.
  • The provider requires password sharing instead of standard user or manager access.
  • Media spend, management fees, and taxes are combined without an auditable breakdown.
  • The provider cannot explain which entity appears in the payments profile.
  • Tracking, analytics, or landing-page assets exist only in provider-controlled systems with no handoff plan.
  • The contract says campaign history or data will disappear when service ends.
  • The provider sells, rents, or rotates advertising accounts.

Questions to put in the agreement

State who controls the client account, payments profile, media payment, data, tags, audiences, creative files, and domains. Define access levels during the engagement, the time allowed for offboarding, the format of exports, and any third-party subscriptions that stop when the service ends. The agreement should also distinguish account access from ownership of original creative or consulting deliverables.

Official Google references

Review Google's current documentation on manager accounts, manager ownership of client accounts, account access levels, and payments profiles. Confirm settings in the live account because interface labels and billing options can vary.
