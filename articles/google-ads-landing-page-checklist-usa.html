US Landing Page Audit

Google Ads landing page checklist for US lead generation

Updated September 28, 2026. Review the complete journey from keyword and ad promise to form submission, sales qualification, and revenue before increasing media spend.

What the page needs to accomplish

A paid-search landing page should quickly confirm that the visitor reached the right business, that the offer matches the search and ad, that important claims can be verified, and that the next step is clear and proportionate. The goal is not to maximize raw form volume. It is to help suitable prospects act while giving sales enough context to qualify them.

Google describes landing-page experience using signals including usefulness, relevance, ease of navigation, the number of links, and whether the page meets expectations created by the ad. These are diagnostic dimensions, not a guarantee of ad rank or conversions.

1. Match the keyword, ad, and first viewport

  • The main headline continues the specific promise made in the ad without copying keywords unnaturally.
  • The service, product, location, audience, and primary action are understandable before scrolling.
  • Pricing cues, eligibility, availability, exclusions, and timing do not contradict the ad.
  • State or metro references are used only where the business genuinely serves those areas.
  • The page does not switch the visitor to a materially different offer after the click.

Compare the search term, ad, and page side by side. A broad homepage often asks the visitor to rediscover the offer; a focused destination can remove that extra decision.

2. Make the business and offer verifiable

Show the legal or trading name, a usable contact path, the actual service scope, and links to privacy and terms information. Explain who the service is for, what is delivered, which work is excluded, and what happens after contact. Use certifications, partner status, case studies, reviews, client logos, guarantees, and performance numbers only when the business has current evidence and permission.

  • Claims identify the conditions and source needed to interpret them.
  • Testimonials are genuine, permissioned, and not presented as typical outcomes without context.
  • Locations and local phone numbers reflect real operations.
  • Fees described on the page agree with the sales process and contract.

3. Reduce form and contact friction

Ask only for information required at the current stage. A high-value or regulated service may reasonably need more qualification than a simple callback request, but every field should have a purpose. Labels, required states, error messages, consent text, and confirmation messages must remain understandable on mobile.

  • Forms accept realistic names, phone formats, and email addresses without avoidable rejection.
  • Phone and email links perform the expected action.
  • The page explains response time without making a promise the team cannot meet.
  • Spam protection does not block normal users or hide an inaccessible challenge.
  • Submission failure preserves entered information where practical and tells the user what to fix.

4. Test mobile usability, accessibility, and speed

Use a real phone as well as browser emulation. Check the page at common narrow widths, with larger text, keyboard navigation, reduced motion, and a slower connection. The primary task should not be covered by a sticky header, chat widget, cookie banner, or pop-up.

  • No horizontal scrolling, clipped headings, overlapping controls, or layout shift during loading.
  • Tap targets are separated and labels remain visible.
  • Images have stable dimensions and useful alternative text where needed.
  • Focus order, contrast, form labels, error identification, and heading order support assistive technology.
  • Critical content loads without requiring a heavy animation or third-party script.

5. Verify every destination and policy path

Test the final URL, redirects, tracking parameters, fragments, sitelinks, call assets, and alternate mobile paths. The display and landing domains must meet Google Ads requirements. Make sure robots directives, authentication, geographic rules, cookie tools, and security systems do not accidentally block Google or normal visitors.

Disapproval should be addressed through the stated policy and appeal process. Do not use cloaking, misleading company details, hidden redirects, or different content for reviewers and customers.

6. Measure meaningful outcomes

Record successful completion rather than clicks on the submit button. Test duplicate prevention, values, currencies, call duration rules, consent states, enhanced conversions where appropriate, and offline lead imports. Preserve campaign identifiers needed to connect leads with opportunities and sales.

  • Primary conversion actions represent outcomes used for bidding.
  • Secondary events remain available for diagnosis without inflating primary results.
  • Sales reports distinguish raw leads, accepted leads, opportunities, sales, and revenue.
  • Privacy disclosures match the actual collection and sharing of data.
  • Quality feedback arrives after the normal sales-cycle delay.

7. Prioritize tests with evidence

Fix broken forms, inaccessible controls, false claims, wrong destinations, missing consent, and unusable mobile layouts before experimenting with button colors or decorative changes. For optimization tests, state the hypothesis, affected traffic, primary outcome, guardrail, minimum observation window, and rollback condition. Avoid changing ads, audiences, bids, tracking, and page design at the same time.

Segment results by campaign intent, device, location, and qualified outcome. A conversion-rate increase can still be harmful if it comes from lower-quality leads or a misleading reduction in qualification.
