US Google Ads Reporting

Google Ads monthly reporting for US businesses

Updated September 29, 2026. A useful report should help a business decide what to keep, stop, investigate, or test next. It should not hide weak measurement behind a large dashboard.

What a monthly report must answer

The first page should answer five questions in plain English: how much was spent, what business outcomes were recorded, how those outcomes compared with the plan, what changed in the account, and what decision is needed next. Impressions, clicks, click-through rate, and average cost per click are useful diagnostic signals, but they are not substitutes for qualified leads, accepted opportunities, sales, revenue, or contribution margin.

Use one reporting period, currency, time zone, attribution basis, and conversion definition throughout. If a number cannot be reconciled, label it as uncertain and state what will be checked.

1. Start with budget and business outcomes

  • Media spend compared with the approved budget and forecast.
  • Primary conversions and cost per conversion, with the conversion definition shown.
  • Qualified leads, opportunities, sales, revenue, and cost per qualified outcome where CRM data exists.
  • Results by campaign, service line, state or metro, device, and new versus returning demand where those splits affect a decision.
  • Material changes from the prior period and from the agreed target, without presenting correlation as proof of cause.

For lead generation, a report should show both platform conversions and downstream quality. Ten form fills are not ten qualified leads. For ecommerce, reported conversion value should be reconciled with order status, refunds, taxes, shipping, and the profit definition used by the business.

2. Explain measurement and conversion lag

Reports can change after the month ends because conversions may occur after the original click. Google Ads conversion windows define how long after an ad interaction a conversion can be recorded. A company with a long sales cycle should not evaluate the newest cohort as though it were mature.

  • List primary and secondary conversion actions and any changes made during the period.
  • State whether enhanced conversions, call tracking, offline conversion imports, or value rules are used.
  • Show the expected delay from click to inquiry, qualification, opportunity, and sale.
  • Reconcile Google Ads, analytics, CRM, and finance using the same dates and definitions.
  • Disclose tracking outages, consent limitations, duplicate events, missing call data, or incomplete sales feedback.

3. Show traffic quality, not only traffic volume

Include a reviewed summary of search terms that spent money, produced qualified demand, or revealed irrelevant intent. Google explains that the search terms report shows the searches that triggered ads and their performance; privacy thresholds can mean it does not expose every query. Treat it as evidence, not a complete transcript.

Separate branded, non-branded, competitor, support, employment, research, and remarketing traffic where relevant. Summarize exclusions added and useful themes discovered. Do not dump personally identifiable query or lead data into a client report.

4. Document management work and account changes

A report should make the service work inspectable. Record meaningful budget reallocations, bid-target changes, campaign launches or pauses, keyword and negative-keyword decisions, ad and asset tests, destination changes, audience exclusions, conversion updates, and policy actions. For each material change, show the date, reason, expected effect, owner, and how the result will be judged.

Google Ads provides account-level, campaign-level, and ad-group reporting, customizable tables, downloads, saved reports, and scheduled delivery. The platform report should support the narrative, while the narrative explains why a change matters to the business.

5. Report tests with a decision rule

List active experiments and changes that are still learning. Each test needs a hypothesis, start date, scope, primary measure, guardrail, minimum evidence requirement, and stop or rollback condition. Avoid declaring a winner from a few clicks or changing several major variables at once.

When evidence is weak, say so. A transparent report can recommend continuing a test, collecting more qualified-lead feedback, or fixing tracking before changing bids. Certainty should match the data.

6. End with decisions and a 30-day plan

  1. Keep: activity that remains aligned with the business goal and has adequate evidence.
  2. Fix: measurement, access, policy, destination, or budget-control defects.
  3. Stop: clearly irrelevant or uneconomic activity, with the evidence recorded.
  4. Test: one prioritized hypothesis with an owner and decision date.
  5. Need from client: product, sales, margin, inventory, compliance, or CRM information required for the next decision.

The report is complete only when the reader knows what will happen next and which uncertainty is still unresolved.

Monthly reporting red flags

  • The headline celebrates clicks while qualified leads or revenue are absent.
  • Definitions, date ranges, attribution settings, or currencies change without explanation.
  • The report contains charts but no account changes, learning, or next action.
  • Every result is described as positive and no limitation is disclosed.
  • The advertiser cannot inspect the underlying account or saved report.
  • Performance is guaranteed despite auction, market, website, sales, and measurement uncertainty.
